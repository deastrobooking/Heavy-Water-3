# Space travel, fighter frames, and arcade battle arenas

**Owner direction — 2026-10-22:** expand the Kestrel into a family of space fighters and build Solar travel, battle levels, and arenas. The attached feature list defines the arcade combat target: fast readable bullet patterns, score chains, close-range graze risk, limited-use bombs, phased bosses, and insect and robot factions. This track is now active alongside the Earth liberation work; it must keep the local four-player save and reward rules.

## Space battles: the design as built (2026-10-24)

**Owner direction — 2026-10-24:** a dedicated space fighter designer with six native designs, and a combat system of tracking missiles, energy machine guns, energy charge blasts and tracking energy bombs; review the space battle and level systems and design the gameplay.

**Review findings that this change fixed:**
- The orbital battle never drew: in space the app replaced every published prop with a fixed set (ring, hub, planet, four cover boulders and the fighter), so enemies, sector backdrops and any shot were invisible. One publisher, `Frontier.publishSpace`, now draws the ring, hub, the sector's backdrop and the battle; the app adds the fighter.
- The fighter's gun was an auto-aimed hit-scan cone, enemies never fired and could only hurt by touching, and nothing used the existing air-war weapon ideas. All six frames flew identically in space (`SpaceFlight` used constants), and the nose followed the drift, so aiming and dodging could not be separated.
- The 40 levels were the same random spawn rule with more enemies; the sectors differed only in tint, every sector was open from the start, and the boss was a large enemy with more health.
- The asteroid "mining operation" paid ore into the shared stockpile while the player waited, which bypassed the colony rule that mined ore belongs to the miner until deposited.

**Controls (twin stick, top-down):** WASD or the left stick move in screen directions; the mouse or right stick turns the aim, and the fighter faces its aim, so it can strafe one way and shoot another. Shift boosts, Alt brakes, Q/E change height (cosmetic: hits are tested on the plane). Click or RT fires the energy machine guns; holding right click charges, releasing fires the charge blast; Z fires a tracking missile; C drops a tracking energy bomb; H starts the next level after a clear; F returns.

**The four weapon systems** (`game/SpaceArsenal.zig`):
| System | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Energy machine guns | Twin bolts from alternating muzzles while fire is held; each shot adds heat; at full heat the guns vent until below 35%. |
| Energy charge blast | Charges while the alternate is held (guns silent while charging); released at 25% or more it fires a piercing blast whose damage and size grow with the charge, eating hostile shots in its path. |
| Tracking missiles | Lock the hostile nearest the aim inside 40° (else the nearest), accelerate and home; the rack reloads one at a time. |
| Tracking energy bombs | A slow homing orb that bursts over an area, damaging every hostile in it and wiping every hostile shot inside the burst; a few per level, one back per cleared wave. |

**The six native frames** (the designer, solar map > FIGHTER DESIGNER): each leans on one system. Kestrel balanced; Ember Wyrm gun interceptor (fastest guns, one bomb); Void Lance charge lancer (0.8 s charge, 220 damage); Dreadclaw missile gunship (8-missile rack, 3.5 s reload); Starwing ion interceptor (fastest, hardest-hitting guns); Bulwark armored bomber (four big bombs, most hull). The designer shows role, speed, agility, hull, gun and charge damage per second, rack and bombs, and each frame's state; choosing an owned frame equips it, choosing the next one unlocks it through the fabricator's existing rules (previous frame, combat rank, P1's wallet). The shared Kestrel upgrades apply to every frame: engine (acceleration and speed), missile rack (+2), gun cooling, armor (hull).

**Enemies fight back, readably:** every attack is announced by its glow and hostile disc flaring before it fires slow, visible orbs. Drones weave in, fire single shots and ram; fighters orbit at 50 m and fire three-shot bursts; transformers fire spreads, then wind up and dash; battleships fire rotating rings plus aimed pairs; the mother ship has three phases by health (rings and drone launches, a spinning spiral, aimed fans with a slow ring), and clears the field at each change. Hits grant 0.7 s of grace. A downed fighter restarts the wave at the relay with half hull.

**Levels** (`game/SpaceLevels.zig`): four sectors on one route, Luna, Mars, the Asteroid Belt and the Kuiper Belt, ten levels each, three waves a level. Each level teaches or mixes one formation (line, pincer, ring, column, escort): drones first, fighters at 3, transformers at 6; level 5 is a battleship midboss and level 10 the mother ship. Each sector has a faction (Luna the Brood, Mars the robot armada, the Belt both, the Kuiper Belt an elite Brood) and a tier that adds health, fire rate, damage and one more of each small craft. A sector opens when the previous one's level 5 is cleared; levels run in order and the boss can be replayed. Clears are shared story flags (`sp_lu1` to `sp_kb10`, plus the sector boss flags).

**Rewards:** each kill pays the pilot's own kit through the air war's loot table (battleships and the mother ship add fixed scrap, parts and XP); a clear pays every present player scrap (and gold for midbosses and bosses). Score multiplies by a kill chain that ends 2.5 s after the last kill or on a hit. The mining run is now played: shoot glowing ore rocks for 2 to 4 ore into the pilot's kit (deposited at a colony store like hand-mined ore), with Brood drones harassing every 25 s.

**Moon surface:** Luna's level-10 rescue flag now opens `LAND ON MOON / SELENE RELAY` in the solar chart and the matching landing row in the local world map. It loads the supplied `assets/Solar System/01_moon_global_16bit.png` through the normal HWMH terrain pipeline as a 60 km by 30 km playable regional map. The foothold is a low-pressure-habitat cluster with protected connector corridors, a landing deck, gold thermal trim, elevated photovoltaic wings and a blue-lit relay mast. The Moon is bare regolith with grey rocks, no forest or Earth mountain panorama. Landing generates a deterministic local ore field centered at Selene; personal hand-mined ore still goes into each player's Kit. Build mode and the existing colony structure system remain available on the site. A separate seeded cave descends to a Hive nest and lunar salvage cache. This is a local terrain slice sampled from the global raster, not a seamless spherical surface; a lunar suit / vacuum survival meter and the broader surface campaign are still future work.

**Not done:** guests (only P1 flies; the plan's couch co-op needs a fighter and controls per seat), the service stop between sectors, the SPACE ROUTES tab inside the world map (the solar map is its own screen), graze scoring, the Robot Forge Ark as a distinct boss pattern (it uses the mother ship's), weapon fine-tuning by hand, and a frame budget measured at the shot caps (96 player, 160 hostile). Mars is not landable yet; Moon survival pressure, a saved/resumable asteroid base with working mining drones, and a full planetary-scale globe remain later work.

## Experience target

Create a dedicated arcade space-flight mode. Keep the Kestrel's atmospheric flight intact. Space steering must be readable from a top-down 3D camera, keep the action in a bounded battle field, and prioritize lateral dodge movement, aim direction, quick acceleration and braking. The first version is local couch co-op; enemy projectiles and the player's shots use fixed-capacity storage and readable shapes/colors.

Navigation is one GUI entry with two explicit tabs: **LOCAL PLANET** and **SPACE ROUTES**. LOCAL PLANET keeps the current globe and regional map, showing cities, destinations, and nearby points of interest on the world currently being explored. SPACE ROUTES shows the solar-system map, visited planets, unlocked battle sectors and their connecting routes. Selecting a space node opens its battle or service stop when that content is playable; locked or planned planets remain visible as unavailable, never as dead-end travel choices. Both tabs must support keyboard, mouse and gamepad, preserve the selected layer on reopen, and clearly mark the current player/world and destination.

A run should move through a short chain of authored orbital battle sectors, with a service/rest stop between sectors. A sector introduces one arena pattern, then mixes patterns, then ends with a giant ship boss. Keep the compact Dogfight square as the tight aerial challenge. Expand the ground battle venue modestly and let a drop-in arena frame the current terrain or city as its backdrop; later venue presets can select a wilds overlook or a city rooftop district. The route map is the travel layer; travel is a choice of connected playable destinations, not a cutscene or list of unreachable worlds.

## Design constraints

- Keep the first playable route short: one service platform, two arenas and one Brood carrier boss. Build a second faction only after the first run is fun and stable.
- Keep ship upgrades and campaign progress shared as they are today; combat score and consumable pickups belong to each player's `Kit` unless a reward explicitly advances shared story.
- Give each projectile a small collision radius, a telegraph, a fixed lifetime, and a clear cancellation rule. A bomb must clear hostile shots and convert them into score/pickups without allowing score farming from one repeated pattern.
- Cap live enemy shots and enemies. When a cap is reached, skip or replace spawns deterministically; never allocate in the frame loop.
- Boss attacks communicate the safe lane and the damage window before firing. A phase transition clears or resolves old bullets before introducing the next pattern.
- The existing giant Scalari mothership meshes are art inputs, not proof of a playable ship. Add landing, collision, boss parts, attacks, and rewards before calling one complete.
- Continue the city loop in parallel in small commits. This Solar track does not change the MVP save separation or add networking.
